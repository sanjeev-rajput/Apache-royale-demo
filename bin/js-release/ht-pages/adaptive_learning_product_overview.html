The central promise: make learning more engaging, more understandable and easier to continue — by bringing learning resources, practice and meaningful feedback into one connected experience.

The experience shown here describes the current proof-of-concept direction. It is not a claim of proven learning gains, automated mastery measurement or a fully personalised learning pathway. Those capabilities require further educational design, validation and development.

Management view · Product proposition

A configurable learning layer for existing education platforms

The opportunity is not simply another quiz or chatbot. It is an adaptable learning experience that can use an institution's curriculum, content catalogue and educator-defined guidance to make learning resources and practice more relevant to each learner.

Content-led

Use your curriculum

Organise learning resources around the subjects, grades, topics and activities that matter to your institution.

Educator-governed

Keep pedagogy configurable

SME-owned profiles guide educational quality, explanations and assessment behaviour.

Evidence-informed

Respond to learner needs

Use assessment and activity evidence to inform recommendations, with appropriate uncertainty.

Integration-minded

Fit into existing systems

Designed as a separable application capability that can be adapted for a publisher or school platform.

The proposition

From content delivery to guided learning journeys

Institutions retain ownership of curriculum and educational policy. The platform coordinates learning context, evidence, AI-assisted interpretation and resource delivery.

Potential value for adopters

Why an education organisation may care

For a school or school network

  • Bring curriculum-aligned resources and practice into a coherent learner experience.
  • Allow educators to guide learning quality through reviewed configuration.
  • Explore evidence-informed recommendations without committing to a fully automated teaching system.

For a publisher or content owner

  • Organise existing digital content by curriculum, grade, topic and activity.
  • Connect content assets to an interactive learning journey.
  • Retain editorial and subject-matter control over educational guidance.

For an EdTech platform

  • Evaluate an adaptive-learning capability that can be integrated with an existing product.
  • Keep subject content and educational rules configurable.
  • Extend the experience without requiring a separate subject-specific engine for every topic.

For product and business teams

  • Start with a bounded proof of concept.
  • Validate learner and educator value before investing in enterprise-scale services.
  • Use the roadmap to plan integration, governance and scale-up.

Educator & SME view · Educational governance

Educational intent stays with the people who understand learning

The platform should not hardcode pedagogy. Subject-matter experts and instructional designers define the educational expectations; the application provides reusable mechanisms to apply them.

What educators and SMEs can govern

Educational areaSME / educator responsibilityHow the platform supports it
Curriculum and contentDefine curriculum alignment, grade suitability, topic structure and approved resources.Catalogue manifest and CSV-based learning catalogues.
Teaching and response styleSpecify age-appropriate language, explanation depth, examples and instructional tone.SME-owned instruction profiles with response guidelines.
Content qualitySet accuracy, clarity, relevance and age-appropriateness expectations.Quality guidelines included in AI generation context.
Assessment designDefine assessment expectations, question quality and feedback guidance.Assessment guidelines supplied to assessment generation.
Diagnosis and interventionDefine how evidence should be interpreted and which learning interventions are suitable.Planned extension of SME profile/policy configuration.
Recommendation policyReview what evidence justifies revision, practice or progression recommendations.Highest-priority configuration change: remove hardcoded recommendation threshold.

Existing configuration foundation

Learning catalogues

CSV-based catalogues currently include English, Mathematics and EVS examples. Catalogue entries can reference an instruction profile through instructionProfileId.

Instruction profiles

JSON profiles include SME ownership and lifecycle metadata such as owner, status, version and review notes, alongside response, quality and assessment guidance.

Language and conversation settings

A separate language configuration holds intent rules, aliases, messages, language behaviour and AI feature settings.

Governance principle

AI-inferred or draft educational guidance must not silently become approved policy. SME review and versioning remain important.

Subject-agnostic by design

The engine should not need a new hardcoded branch for every subject. Subject differences should be described through curriculum data, learning objectives when available, evidence expectations and SME guidance.

Illustrative subjectPossible evidenceSME-defined interpretation
MathematicsWorked steps, selected answer, numerical result, explanation.Whether the learner applied a concept or procedure correctly.
ScienceExplanation, observation, classification, application.Whether the learner can explain or apply a scientific idea.
English / languagesReading response, grammar choice, written or spoken response.Whether the learner demonstrates the targeted language skill.
Social StudiesInterpretation, chronology, source-based response, reasoning.Whether the learner can interpret information and support a conclusion.

Examples are illustrative. The current POC does not claim to support every evidence type above. Additional response formats and evaluation methods would need to be implemented and validated.

How to avoid misleading learner conclusions

  • An incorrect response is evidence, not automatic proof of a misconception.
  • Insufficient evidence must be an acceptable outcome.
  • Mastery criteria and recommendation bands must be reviewed by the relevant SME.
  • Feedback should be appropriate to the specific learning objective and learner context.
  • The learner should be offered helpful choices rather than forced into a rigid path.
For subject experts, curriculum leads, assessment designers and instructional designers.
Technology view · Architecture & integration

A modular capability designed for integration

The current POC uses Apache Royale for its frontend and Node.js for backend orchestration and services. The architecture direction keeps educational configuration separate from application mechanics and AI-provider details.

Conceptual responsibility boundaries

LayerResponsibilityOwnership / change pattern
Experience layerAdaptive learner interface and presentation of activities, resources and assessments.Frontend implementation; integrate into host platform UI as required.
Adaptive orchestrationSession lifecycle, topic context, action handling and coordination of Adaptive workflows.Application code; keep isolated from Agentic and AI K12 experiences.
Catalogue and resource servicesResolve available learning content and resource metadata.Catalogue data and service logic.
Configuration servicesLoad catalogue manifests, language settings and SME instruction profiles.Configuration-driven; validate schema and lifecycle status.
Assessment servicesGenerate, validate, verify and score assessments using server-owned records.Adaptive-specific assessment path; deterministic score authority.
LLM provider layerAbstract model/provider selection and generation requests.Central provider management; model-agnostic application design.

Configuration versus code

Configuration / SME

Educational variation

  • Curriculum, subject, grade, topic and catalogue metadata.
  • Response, quality and assessment guidelines.
  • Diagnostic, mastery and recommendation policies as these are introduced.
  • Language, intent phrases, aliases and conversation messages.
Code / engineering

Reusable mechanics

  • Load and validate configuration.
  • Manage sessions and evidence contracts.
  • Call services and models; handle timeouts and failures.
  • Enforce schema integrity, permissions and deterministic scoring.

Integration approach for an adopting organisation

Option A · Integrate capability

Potential

Adopt selected backend services and the learner experience, then connect them to the organisation's identity, content and platform services.

Option B · Adapt the codebase

Potential

Use the POC as a reference implementation and adapt frontend, APIs, data contracts and operational controls to the host architecture.

Option C · Use as a product foundation

Potential

Extend the capability into a more complete solution with enterprise identity, persistence, monitoring, governance and deployment architecture.

Integration work to assess

Per adopter

Authentication, authorisation, content APIs, learner records, privacy, data retention, hosting, security reviews, load and operational support.

These are possible adoption patterns, not currently delivered deployment packages or contractual offers. Suitability depends on code review, licensing, security and integration assessment.

Engineering guardrails

  • Do not hardcode subject names, topic names or educational thresholds in central Adaptive orchestration.
  • Do not allow an LLM to authoritatively set marks, answer keys or learner records.
  • Keep non-LLM resource and Question Bank functionality available when an LLM is unavailable.
  • Keep Adaptive, Agentic and AI K12 workflows separated unless a deliberate shared-service decision is approved.
  • Validate configuration structure and fail safely with useful diagnostics.
  • Do not claim a source-level check is equivalent to a full browser, security or load test.
For software architects, engineering teams, integration partners, security reviewers and technical evaluators.
Roadmap view · Maturity & next steps

Build the right foundation before scaling the promise

The roadmap deliberately prioritises educational correctness and configuration governance before adding complex learner analytics or enterprise infrastructure.

P0 · Highest priority: make recommendations SME-configurable

The current Adaptive recommendationsFor() contains a hardcoded 60% comparison. Move the educational decision into reviewed configuration, test boundary and missing-policy behaviour, and do not assume 60% is pedagogically valid without SME approval.

P0 · Recommendation policy

Immediate

Remove hardcoded educational threshold and mapping. Resolve policy source and safe fallback.

Acceptance: changing the approved configuration changes recommendations without modifying JavaScript.

P1 · Lightweight evidence contract

Next

Represent assessment and activity evidence in a common session-scoped format while preserving source-specific details.

Acceptance: AI and Question Bank evidence can be used consistently without losing provenance.

P2 · SME diagnostic guidance

Planned

Extend existing profiles where justified; audit and reuse misconception detection; support insufficient evidence.

Acceptance: no subject-specific diagnostic branches in the generic engine.

P3 · Evidence-informed suggestions

Planned

Connect evidence and approved policies to learner-selectable activities, explanations, videos and assessments.

Acceptance: recommendations have a clear rationale and do not force a learner path.

P4 · Learning objective metadata

Future scale

Add optional objective identifiers and relationships when catalogue owners and SMEs are ready to maintain them.

Acceptance: new subjects and topics are primarily data/configuration additions.

P5 · Persistent learner profile

Beyond POC

Consider cross-session progress after identity, storage, privacy, consent and retention are designed.

Acceptance: separate product and architecture approval before implementation.
